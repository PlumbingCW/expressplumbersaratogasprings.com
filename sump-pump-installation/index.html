Emergency Sump Pump Repair in Saratoga Springs, UT

A sump pump that fails during a storm or a wet spell leaves water with nowhere to go. Our plumbers repair and install sump and ejector pumps in Saratoga Springs.

Sump pump installation and repair in Saratoga Springs, UT covers the pump that sits in a basin below the floor and pushes water out of the house, plus the check valve, float switch and discharge line around it. A failed pump lets water rise in the pit and across the floor. Our 24/7 emergency line takes calls at any hour when a pump stops or a pit is overflowing.

While you wait, shut off power to anything standing in water only if the panel is dry, move items off the floor, and keep out of rising water. Nights, weekends and same-day calls are dispatched as the schedule allows, and we can install a replacement pump or a battery backup once the water is under control.

Water in the basement and the pit looks full: start here

If you see water in the pit or on the floor, work out what kind of water it is. Plumbing water comes from a supply line or a drain. Groundwater comes through the floor or walls. A sump pit handles groundwater, and a sewage ejector basin handles wastewater from basement fixtures.

These two systems look similar and are not interchangeable. An ejector basin that overflows is sewage, so keep people away. A sump that overflows is groundwater, which is cleaner but can still carry soil and bacteria.

What to look for in the pit

  • The pump runs constantly and the water level does not fall
  • The pump does not start even though the pit is full
  • The motor hums but nothing discharges
  • The pump cycles on and off every few seconds
  • A smell of sewage near an ejector basin

When a sump pump failure is an emergency

A pump that stops in the middle of a wet period turns a damp floor into standing water quickly. Finished basements, stored boxes and furnaces or water heaters at floor level are at risk.

Call the 24/7 line if the pit is at the rim, if the pump will not start, or if sewage is rising from an ejector basin. If you can do it safely, check the breaker and make sure the float is not stuck. After-hours and weekend calls get the same priority as daytime ones, and a temporary pump may get the water down while the repair is done.

An old, noisy or slow pump is a candidate for replacement before the wet season.

How to test your pump before it matters

Test a sump pump a few times a year and before a wet season. It takes five minutes.

  1. Check the powerMake sure the pump is plugged into a working outlet and the breaker is on.
  2. Fill the pitPour a few buckets of water slowly into the basin until the float rises.
  3. Watch the cycleThe pump should start, empty the pit and shut off cleanly.
  4. Listen and lookCheck for grinding, loud humming, and water falling back after the cycle.

Battery backup and the discharge line

A battery backup pump takes over when power fails or the main pump quits. It is a smart addition for a pit that matters, because storms often cut power just when the pump is needed. Batteries need replacing and testing like any other.

The discharge line carries water away from the foundation. It should run well clear of the house and slope away. A line that empties at the foundation or freezes shut sends water back to the pit. Ask the Saratoga Springs building department about permit rules before installing new discharge piping, since rules vary by job.

Sump pump versus sewage ejector pump

A sump pump moves groundwater from a pit. A sewage ejector pump lifts wastewater from a basement bathroom or laundry up to the main drain, and it handles solids. The pump, basin and cover are different.

If you have a basement bathroom and the toilet will not flush, or you smell sewage near the basin, it is the ejector. Replacing an ejector is a different job from replacing a sump pump, and we match the pump to the use.

Installing a new sump pump

  1. Choose the locationThe pit goes at the lowest point or where the water collects, with room for the float.
  2. Set the basinWe cut the slab, dig the pit and set a basin with a lid.
  3. Install the pump and check valveThe pump sits on a firm base with the check valve on the discharge pipe.
  4. Run the dischargeWe run the pipe to the outside and away from the foundation.
  5. TestWe fill the pit and run a full cycle before leaving.

If you have a pre-existing pit, replacing the pump alone is often possible. Ask us to look first.
