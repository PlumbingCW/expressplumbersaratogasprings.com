Emergency Water Line Repair in Saratoga Springs, UT

The pipe from the meter to your house is out of sight until it fails. Our plumbers repair and replace water lines in Saratoga Springs, with 24/7 dispatch for leaks.

Updated 2026-10-15 · Written by the Saratoga Springs Express Plumbing team

Water line repair and replacement in Saratoga Springs, UT covers the underground pipe that carries water from the meter or main to your house, including finding a leak, repairing a section or replacing the whole line. If your lawn is flooding, pressure has dropped or the meter will not stop, our 24/7 emergency line is open.

While you wait for a plumber, close the main valve where the line enters the house and keep people away from any soft or sinking ground. Do not dig near the line yourself. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and weekend and overnight calls reach the same dispatch line.

Who owns which pipe: the meter, the street and your side

Start with the boundary. A broken City water main in the street is a City matter, so call Saratoga Springs Public Works for it. The pipe from the meter to the house may belong to the homeowner, which means a leak on that stretch is private plumbing.

Utilities generally do not mark lines they do not own, a point the Blue Stakes guide makes about locating. If you are unsure, tell the dispatcher what you see and we will help sort out the side. The city's Water Division page is a good place to start for the street side.

When a water line leak is an emergency

Some service line leaks are slow. Others turn a yard into a mud pit or empty the pressure from the house. These signs mean act now, whatever the hour.

Call the 24/7 line right away if you see

  • Water bubbling up in the lawn, driveway or planter strip with no sprinklers running
  • A meter that spins fast after the house valve is closed
  • A sudden drop in pressure with a wet spot outside
  • Sinking, soft or muddy ground along the path of the line
  • Dirty or discolored water after a break

Close the house shutoff and note the meter reading. If water is rushing out and you cannot reach the valve, tell the dispatcher so a plumber can plan for it. Night or weekend, the call is the same. Keep kids and pets off wet or soft ground.

Finding the leak before anyone digs

A wet lawn tells you the water surfaces, not where the pipe is broken. The water can travel along the trench or soil layers before it appears. We start with a meter test and a pressure test on the line, then use listening gear and a pipe locator to trace it.

Our leak detection page lists the tools in more detail. The goal is to dig one small hole in the right place, not a trench along the whole line.

Low water pressure: causes to check before blaming the line

Low pressure can come from many things, and a bad water line is only one. Check these first, because they are cheaper to fix.

A partly closed valve at the meter or house is the most common. A failing pressure reducer can pass too little pressure. A corroded or leaking line cuts flow, and a clogged fixture screen or shared fixture problem affects one tap but not others.

Questions that narrow down low pressure

  • Is the house shutoff and meter valve fully open?
  • Is the low pressure on every faucet or only one?
  • Is it worse on hot water only, which points to the heater?
  • Did it begin suddenly, which suggests a break?
  • Is there a wet spot outside, which suggests a leak?

If every tap is weak and the yard is wet, the service line is the suspect. If one fixture is weak, look at the faucet or its supply line. We check the pressure reducer, valves and line, then tell you the cause. We do not state a pressure number here because it depends on your home and your supply.

Repair or replace: how we decide

A single leak on a good line is a spot repair. A line with repeated leaks, corrosion or a pipe type that is failing is a replacement. The decision depends on the evidence we find.

We show you the failed section and explain what we see. A short section is cheaper to replace than to dig up again in a year.

Trench or trenchless: the two ways to replace a line

Trench replacement means digging along the old line and laying new pipe. It is direct and lets us inspect the ground, but it disturbs the lawn or hardscape. Trenchless replacement means digging at each end and pulling or pushing new pipe through the old path with minimal surface work.

Trenchless suits a line under a driveway or a mature landscape. It is not right for every soil or every line, so we explain which method fits your yard. Restoring sod, concrete and landscaping is separate work from the pipe.

Before the ground is opened: 811 and Blue Stakes

Call 811 at least three business days before any digging. Blue Stakes of Utah arranges for utility lines to be marked, but the utilities do not mark lines they do not own. That means your private water line may not be marked by the call.

We locate and trace the line ourselves before digging, and we plan around gas, power and other lines the call does mark. Do not dig on your own, since a nick to the wrong line is dangerous.

Shutting off water and keeping culinary and irrigation separate

Know where your main valve is before a break. If a repair needs the meter turned off, we coordinate it. The City handles the meter and its valve.

Saratoga Springs also runs a separate pressurized irrigation system, and cross-connections between culinary and irrigation lines are illegal. Irrigation water is non-potable. We never tie the two together, and a new water line repair must keep them separate. The irrigation connection is the green box marked irrigation near your property line, and it is not part of the house line we repair.

Water main replacement and larger projects

A water main replacement on a street belongs to the city, not to a private plumber. On private property, we replace the service line from the meter to the home. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Saratoga Springs building department before work starts, and we will tell you what we can about the scope.

What affects the price of a water line job

Length and depth

A short line near the surface costs less than a long, deep one.

Trench or trenchless

Trenchless uses special equipment and less restoration. Trench work needs more digging and surface repair.

Surface restoration

Lawn, driveway, concrete and landscaping repair may be separate trades and costs.

Pipe material

Different pipe types use different parts, fittings and methods.

Emergency dispatch

Night, weekend and holiday calls are available at any hour. Ask for the clear price before work starts.

A note on insurance

Homeowners insurance usually does not cover the repair of the line that failed from age or wear. Some policies offer service line coverage as an add-on. Check yours, and ask whether resulting damage is covered.
