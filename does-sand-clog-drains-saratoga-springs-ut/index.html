Does Sand Clog Drains? Saratoga Springs, UT Lake-Day Guide

A pinch of sand rinses through most drains. A bucket-load from lake gear settles in traps, floor drains and the bottom of a water heater, and stays there.

Drains · 6 min read · Updated 2026-10-15 · Written by the Saratoga Springs Express Plumbing team

Call (385) 488-1892

Sand can clog a drain, but usually only when a lot goes down at once or settles where water moves slowly. A pinch from a swimsuit rinses through a shower trap. A scoop from a beach bag or a cooler settles in the trap and the first flat stretch of pipe. Sand is heavy and does not dissolve, so it builds a layer that traps hair and soap. Lake days make it worse.

This guide covers where sand goes, a rinsing routine for lake days and what to do when it has already built up.

Where sand ends up in a house

Most of these are small habits. A drain that already runs slow needs a cable, not a new habit.

A rinse routine for lake days

  1. Shake and brushKnock the sand off towels, shoes and bags on the lawn or driveway before they come in.
  2. Rinse outdoorsHose off feet, strollers and gear on a bed of gravel or grass rather than into an indoor drain.
  3. Use the strainersKeep a fine mesh strainer in the shower and in the utility sink and empty it into the trash, not down the drain.
  4. Flush with waterIf a little sand does go down, run plenty of water for a minute so it clears the trap instead of settling.

Outdoor showers and floor drains

An outdoor shower is convenient after a day at the lake, and it is also a trap for sand. Its drain is often small and shallow. Put a gravel bed or a removable screen over it, and scoop sediment out by hand every season.

Floor drains in garages and laundry rooms catch what drips off boats and coolers. They have a trap that holds water, and sand piles up there. Pull the grate, vacuum or scoop out the grit, and pour in water to refill the trap.

Sediment in the water heater

Water heaters collect sediment at the bottom of the tank over time. It is mostly minerals from the supply, not lake sand, but it behaves the same way. It forms a layer, makes the burner or element work harder and can cause popping sounds when it heats.

A yearly flush through the drain valve helps. If sediment is heavy or the valve is clogged, have a plumber check the tank. Lake-day habits do not affect the heater, so keep this task separate from the rinse routine.

Why sand is harder on drains than most debris

Hair, paper and soap move with the water, and a good flush carries them along. Sand sinks. In a horizontal pipe it drops to the bottom the moment the flow slows, and the next load stacks on top of it.

A drain with a low spot or a slight sag collects sand first. That is why a floor drain or a garage line, which sees little flow, can fill with grit while the shower drain a few feet away runs fine.

Laundry day after the lake

Swimsuits, towels and kids' clothes carry more sand than you can see. A washer pumps all of it into the standpipe in one burst. Shake each item hard outside, and rinse swimwear with a hose before it goes in the machine.

If the washer drain gurgles or backs up into the laundry sink after a lake weekend, sand is a reasonable first guess. Stop using the washer and have the line checked, since a full standpipe can overflow onto the floor.

Signs sand has already built up

What to look for

  • A tub or shower that drains slowly even after you remove hair
  • A gurgle from the tub when the washer drains
  • A gritty feel when you scrape the inside of the trap
  • A floor drain that smells or holds water longer than usual
  • Sand showing up in the bottom of the strainer every time you use it

What a plumber does about sand

A cable machine can break up a sand and hair plug and pull it out. For a larger deposit in a branch line, hydro-jetting flushes the sand out of the pipe. Our drain cleaning team handles tub, laundry and floor drain clogs.

If sand is reaching the main, or several drains back up at once, sewer line cleaning clears the longer run to the street. We can run a camera after to check for a low spot where sand keeps collecting.

Saratoga Springs: Utah Lake days and what comes home with you

Saratoga Springs sits on the northwestern shore of Utah Lake, and the Saratoga Springs Marina and Pelican Point on the lake's west side are two named places people head for a day on the water. Whatever grit comes home on towels and boat gear, give it a rinse spot on the lawn or driveway before it reaches an indoor drain.

Set up that spot once. A hose bib, a gravel bed and a bucket handle most of it, and the result is a lot fewer service calls for sand.
