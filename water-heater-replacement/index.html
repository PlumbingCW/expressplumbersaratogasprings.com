A failed tank: what to do in the first hour

A tank that fails does not stop politely. Water flows across the floor until the supply is closed, and a tank holding 40 to 50 gallons can soak a finished garage in minutes. The first hour is about stopping the water and keeping the area safe.

Do this before we arrive

  • Close the cold water shutoff above the heater, or the main valve if that one is stuck
  • Turn off the breaker for an electric heater or the gas control for a gas heater
  • Move boxes, cardboard and electronics off the floor
  • Photograph the tank, the label and the water for your insurer

Tell the dispatcher the tank's capacity, fuel type and approximate age if the label is readable. That lets the plumber plan for the right replacement the first time.

Choosing the next water heater: tank, tankless or hybrid

Most homeowners replace like for like because it is fast and the existing gas line, vent or wiring already fits. That is a good default. If you are ready to change fuel or style, the choice is worth ten minutes.

Switching from gas to a heat pump water heater

A heat pump water heater pulls heat from the air around it, which means it needs room. A small closet can starve it of air and chill the space. A garage or utility room with open volume works better, and it needs a drain for condensate and a dedicated electrical circuit.

Converting from gas also means capping the gas line properly and handling the old vent so it does not leak air into the home. We walk through the gas shutoff, the electrical load and the condensate path before quoting. Ask the Saratoga Springs building department which permit applies to a fuel change before the job starts.

Sizing a replacement

Capacity should match how your household uses hot water, not just the tank you had before. Look at the number of people, whether two showers run at once and whether you have a large soaking tub. A first-hour rating on the yellow EnergyGuide label says more than the gallon count alone.

An undersized heater runs out during the morning rush. An oversized one wastes standby heat and costs more. We size by usage and by what fits the space, then check that the gas line, venting or breaker can carry the load.

Expansion tank, strapping, drain pan and discharge

Four small items decide whether a new heater lasts. An expansion tank absorbs the thermal swelling of heated water in a closed system, and without it the T&P valve may vent often. A drain pan with a piped drain protects the floor if the tank ever weeps again.

The discharge pipe from the T&P valve must run to a safe spot, and we never cap it. Utah emergency guidance says to secure your water heater to the wall, since a tank that shifts in an earthquake can tear its gas or water connections. Strapping is a safety step we add, and we do not present it as a code claim.

What a replacement visit looks like

  1. Shut off and drainWe close water, power and fuel, attach a hose and empty the tank.
  2. RemoveThe old tank is disconnected, moved out and hauled away.
  3. Set and connectThe new heater goes on a pan. We connect water with new flexible lines, set the vent or electrical connection and install the T&P discharge.
  4. Fill and testWe fill the tank, purge air, light or power it, check for leaks and confirm the temperature setting.
  5. Walk-throughYou see the shutoff valve, the thermostat and the drain valve so you know where they are.

Most replacements fit in a single visit. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Saratoga Springs building department before work starts.

What affects the price of water heater replacement

Type and capacity

A 40 gallon standard tank costs far less than a tankless or a heat pump model.

Fuel and conversion

Changing from gas to electric, or the other way, adds line, venting or electrical work.

Venting and gas line

A new vent run or a longer gas line adds parts and time.

Code items and extras

Expansion tank, pan, piped discharge, new shutoff valves and strapping all add to the quote.

Access and haul-away

Attic installs and stairs take longer. Old tank removal is part of the job.

A note on insurance

Homeowners insurance may pay for water damage from a sudden tank failure, but it generally does not pay for the heater itself or for replacing it because of age. Ask your insurer before you file.
