Underground Water Leak Detection in Saratoga Springs, UT

A buried leak is found with listening gear, pressure tests and locators, not by guessing and digging. Here is how each method works and what to do before any trench opens.

Leaks · 7 min read · Updated 2026-10-15 · Written by the Saratoga Springs Express Plumbing team

Call (385) 488-1892

Underground water leak detection in Saratoga Springs, UT uses listening devices, pressure tests, tracer gas, a pipe locator and sometimes a thermal camera to find a buried leak before anyone digs. The goal is a spot the size of a dinner plate, not a trench across the yard. Which method works depends on the pipe material, the soil and how much noise the leak makes.

This guide lays out the methods in a table, then covers what comes after the leak is found, the signs that point to a buried leak, and why you should call 811 before any digging.

Signs that a leak is under the ground

What a buried leak looks like from the surface

  • Water meter keeps moving after you shut every tap off
  • A wet or sunken patch of lawn when it has not rained
  • Warm spots on a slab floor, or a hot-water line that runs a long time before the water warms
  • Sound of running water in a quiet house
  • A drop in water pressure at every fixture
  • A bill that climbs without a change in how you use water

How the methods differ

Buried leak detection methods and their limits
MethodWhat it findsLimits
Acoustic listeningThe hiss or hum of water escaping a pressurized pipePlastic pipe and soft soil carry sound poorly, and traffic noise masks it
Pressure testWhether a section of pipe holds pressure when isolatedShows that a leak exists, not exactly where it is
Tracer gasGas pumped into the empty line escapes at the break and is sniffed at the surfaceNeeds the line isolated and drained first
Pipe locatorThe path and depth of a buried lineLocates the pipe, not the leak, so it pairs with another method
Thermal imagingTemperature differences from a leaking hot line or wet slabWorks best for hot water leaks and surface-near water
Camera inspectionCracks and breaks inside drain and sewer linesCovers waste pipes, not pressurized supply lines

Plumbers usually combine two methods to confirm a spot before digging.

A locate from first call to marked spot

  1. Confirm the leakThe plumber repeats the meter test with the main valve open and closed to separate house plumbing from the buried line.
  2. Trace the pipeA pipe locator follows the line from the meter toward the house, so the search covers the right strip of ground.
  3. Listen and testListening gear goes on valves and the ground, and a pressure test isolates the section that loses pressure.
  4. Mark and confirmThe spot is marked with paint or flags, and a second method confirms it before any digging.

Hot and cold lines behave differently. A hot water line leaks warm water that a thermal camera can see, while a cold line leak depends on sound and pressure. If only the hot side loses pressure, tell the plumber so the search starts there.

What happens after the leak is found

Once the spot is marked, you have three questions: how bad is the damage, how long is the damaged section, and how can it be reached? A short break may be repaired with a trench of a few feet. A long run of failing pipe is a different decision.

That decision is between water line repair and replacing more of the line, and we show you what was found before work starts. If leaks keep recurring, a longer section is often the better answer than a string of spot repairs.

Why you call 811 before the shovel goes in

Blue Stakes of Utah asks you to call 811 at least three business days before you dig. Buried gas, electric and communication lines can sit close to a water line, and a missed strike is dangerous and expensive.

The marking covers the lines the utilities own. The pipe from the meter to your house may belong to you, and utilities do not mark lines they do not own. A plumber can use a pipe locator to trace your own line, so ask us about that before digging.

Slab leaks and why they need a different plan

A leak under a concrete slab behaves like a buried leak, but the options differ because the pipe is under the floor. Listening gear on the floor and a thermal camera on the slab narrow the location, and the plumber then decides whether to open the slab or reroute the line overhead.

What changes the bill for leak detection

Number of methods

A single listening pass is quicker than a pressure test plus tracer gas. A stubborn leak takes more than one method.

Access

Clear lawn is easy. A leak under a driveway, patio or finished slab takes more time to reach.

Pipe material and age

Plastic pipe transmits sound poorly, so the plumber may rely more on pressure and gas methods.

Repair afterward

Detection and repair are separate steps. Ask for the detection result in writing before you approve the repair.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Close the main shutoff if water is surfacing or the meter is racing. Photograph the wet area and the meter, and note when you first saw it. Mark any sprinkler lines you know of with flags so the crew avoids them.

Keep children and pets off soft, wet ground, because a saturated patch can hide a void where the soil has washed away. Do not dig it out yourself. Opening the wrong spot can damage the pipe or a nearby cable.
