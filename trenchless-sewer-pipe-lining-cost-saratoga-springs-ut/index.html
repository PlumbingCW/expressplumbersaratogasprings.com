Trenchless Sewer Pipe Lining Cost in Saratoga Springs, UT

National guides put trenchless sewer repair at roughly $60 to $250 per foot. Your number depends on a camera inspection, the pipe's condition and how much digging is still needed.

Sewer repair · 8 min read · Updated 2026-10-15 · Written by the Saratoga Springs Express Plumbing team

Call (385) 488-1892

Published national guides put trenchless sewer repair at about $60 to $250 per foot. Pipe lining, also called cured-in-place pipe, runs about $80 or $90 up to $250 per foot depending on the source. Those are national ranges from named websites, not quotes from us and not Utah prices. Your real cost depends on pipe length, condition and access. We show where each range comes from and why a camera comes first.

We do not publish a price list. This page shows where each range comes from, how the three main repair methods compare and why we run a camera before we say a word about price.

HomeAdvisor and Angi both list $4 to $12 per foot just to dig a trench, which excludes the pipe, labor and restoring the yard.

Why the camera comes first

A sewer camera inspection shows what the pipe is, how long it runs, where it is cracked and whether it can take a liner. HomeAdvisor lists a video sewer inspection at $100 to $500 nationally. That is a small part of the total and it prevents a wrong guess.

  1. Clear the lineRoots and scale must be cut out so the camera and a liner can pass. A sewer line repair decision comes after, not before.
  2. Run the cameraWe push a camera down the cleanout and record the pipe. A sewer camera inspection shows cracks, offsets, bellies, roots and the pipe material.
  3. Locate the defectA locator on the surface marks the depth and position of each defect so the repair is planned from facts.
  4. Choose the methodWith the video in hand, you and the plumber compare lining, bursting and trench for your pipe.

When lining does not work

Signs lining may not be the answer

  • The pipe has collapsed or is crushed, since a liner needs an open channel to fit inside
  • A deep sag or belly holds water, because a liner follows the shape of the pipe and will not remove the sag
  • A severe offset at a joint, which the liner cannot bridge cleanly
  • A pipe that is already small, because the liner reduces the inside diameter slightly
  • Heavy root growth that cannot be fully cleared first

What moves the price up or down

Length of the run

Per-foot pricing means a long line costs much more than a short one.

Number of access points

Each pit or cleanout adds labor and restoration.

Pipe condition

Clearing roots and prepping the pipe takes time before any liner goes in.

Under hardscape

A line under a driveway or slab is harder to reach by any method.

Permits and restoration

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Saratoga Springs building department. Landscaping and surface work add to the total.

What the per-foot ranges mean for a sample line

Here is arithmetic, not a quote. A 50-foot line priced at the national trenchless range of $60 to $250 per foot works out to $3,000 to $12,500 before any extras. A 100-foot line doubles both ends.

The spread is wide because the ranges blend easy jobs and hard ones. That is why the camera, the defect map and a written scope matter more than any national average.

How to compare quotes fairly

Ask each contractor for

  • The method named in the scope: lining, bursting or open trench
  • The length of pipe covered, with footage written down
  • Whether root cutting and pipe cleaning are included before the liner goes in
  • Where access pits are dug and who restores the surface
  • Whether a post-repair camera video is provided

Overall sewer replacement averages

For context, HomeAdvisor reports an average of $2,600 for a main sewer replacement nationally, with most homeowners spending $1,388 to $5,323. That is not split by method, so it does not tell you whether lining would be cheaper for your pipe.

Saratoga Springs: who handles what on the sewer system

Saratoga Springs Public Works operates the sanitary sewer collection system, and Timpanogos Special Service District treats the sewage. The city's page says backups inside buildings are generally caused by blockages in the building's own piping, so camera your own line before paying for a repair that may not be needed.

The city page does not say who owns the lateral, so ask Public Works before assuming. If any repair needs digging, call 811 at least three business days ahead through Blue Stakes of Utah, since utilities do not mark lines they do not own.
