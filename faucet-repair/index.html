Emergency Faucet Repair in Saratoga Springs, UT

A shutoff valve that will not close turns a small drip into a flood. We repair faucets, valves and supply lines in Saratoga Springs, day or night.

Updated 2026-10-15 · Written by the Saratoga Springs Express Plumbing team

Call (385) 488-1892

Faucet and sink repair in Saratoga Springs, UT means fixing dripping or stiff faucets, leaking shower and tub valves, failed supply lines and the shutoff valves that control them. Our licensed plumbers run a 24/7 emergency line, so a valve that will not shut off at midnight is answered like any weekday call. Most fixture leaks come from a worn cartridge, washer or seal, and replacing that part often beats replacing the faucet.

If water will not stop, close the main water valve for the house, usually near the water heater or where the line enters the building. Then open the lowest faucet to relieve pressure. Put towels and a bucket under the leak. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, including weekends.

When a valve that will not shut off is an emergency

A fixture shutoff that spins freely, stays stuck or keeps leaking after it is closed is an urgent problem. You cannot repair a faucet or supply line safely when the water cannot be stopped, and old valves can break when forced.

Treat as an emergency if

  • Water sprays or pours from a braided supply line under a sink
  • The shutoff valve handle turns without any resistance
  • A faucet keeps running after the shutoff is closed
  • Water drips from the valve stem even with the handle tight
  • Cabinet floor or ceiling below is wet or swollen

Close the main water valve for the house, then call. We bring replacement quarter-turn valves and braided lines, shut off upstream if the valve fails and replace it so the fixture works again. For a leak that has already reached drywall or flooring, tell the dispatcher so the right tools come first.

Broken or stuck shutoff valves under sinks and behind toilets

Shutoff valves sit unused for years and mineral deposits freeze the stem. A multi-turn valve may also have a washer that has crumbled, so it turns but never seals. These valves also carry the weight of a connected supply line, and an old compression fitting can crack when it is moved.

We replace the valve and the line in one visit. Newer quarter-turn ball valves close fully with a short turn and are easier to use when a fixture fails. The same valves feed toilets, so a stuck one in the bathroom is a candidate for the same fix.

Dripping, stiff or weak faucets

A drip wastes water and usually means a worn internal part. Single-handle faucets use a cartridge, ball or disc that wears at the seals. Two-handle faucets use washers, stems or seats. A stripped or seized cartridge, which several callers describe as stuck, often needs a puller tool.

Weak flow is usually a clogged aerator or a partially closed valve, and sometimes a corroded cartridge. We check flow at the faucet before blaming the line. Low pressure at every fixture is a different problem, and we cover it under the main supply.

Shower valve and showerhead leaks

A showerhead that drips after you turn it off usually points to the cartridge behind the wall. A showerhead that leaks at the arm often needs plumber's tape or a new washer. Water behind the wall, a stain on the next room's ceiling or a valve that sprays from the handle needs a plumber before the leak spreads.

Bathtub faucet repair

A dripping tub spout is easy to ignore until the bill arrives or the drip stains the tub. Most tub faucets fail the same way as shower valves: a worn cartridge, stem or diverter. The valve is in the wall, so we often work through an access panel or the shower side.

Treat a tub faucet that will not stop, runs hot and cold at once or leaks behind the wall as urgent. These repairs also check whether the water heater is delivering mixed water too hot to the fixture, and when it is, we send the problem to water heater repair.

Shower and tub drains that will not clear

A tub that stays full or a shower that pools around your ankles is usually hair and soap in the trap, not a faucet problem. Our drain cleaning service cables the line, and we ask whether other fixtures gurgle, because that points to a shared branch instead of one drain.

Spigots, utility sinks and jetted tubs

Outdoor spigots fail from worn packing, cracked bodies or hoses left attached, and a dripping hose bib often shows up in spring. Saratoga Springs also has a separate non-potable pressurized irrigation system, charged around April 15, drained the week of October 15 and shut off at the individual valve after November 1. Do not fill drinking containers from any line that connects to that system.

We confirm which system a leaking spigot is on before we touch it, since irrigation and drinking lines must not be cross-connected. A laundry or utility sink leaks at the faucet, drain trap or supply connection, and the fix is usually simple. Jetted tubs are different. We handle the water and drain connections, but the pump motor and its electrical parts are best sent to the tub's manufacturer or an electrician.

What affects the price of fixture repair

Repair or replacement

A cartridge, washer or valve is a small parts job. A new faucet or a new tub spout adds the fixture.

Access

A sink with open space under it is easier than a shower valve behind a tiled wall.

Parts and finish

Common brands and finishes use stocked parts, and rare or older cartridges may need to be ordered.

Valve and line condition

A seized shutoff or corroded supply line adds replacement work before the faucet can be fixed.

Time of day

Nights, weekends and holidays are answered the same way. Ask for the clear price before work starts.

A note on insurance

Homeowners insurance typically covers sudden water damage when a supply line or valve fails, but the repair of the fixture itself and slow drips over months are usually your cost. Keep the failed part if the plumber can save it, because an adjuster may ask to see it.
