When the water is lukewarm, cold in bursts or showing a code

People rarely call about a tankless unit by name. They call because the shower runs warm instead of hot, or turns cold for a few seconds in the middle, or the display shows a number they cannot decode. Each symptom has a short list of causes.

Signs your tankless unit needs service

  • Hot water that never gets quite hot enough
  • A display code, with or without a beep
  • Hot water that cuts out mid-shower
  • Scale flakes in an aerator or inlet screen
  • A unit more than a year past its last flush

Flush and descale: how, why and when

Scale is the main cause of tankless trouble. Dissolved minerals in the water leave a crust inside the heat exchanger when water is heated, and the crust narrows the passages and slows heat transfer. The unit then works harder and sometimes shuts off on a high temperature fault.

A flush runs a descaling solution, typically a mild acid, through the unit using a small pump and hoses connected to its service valves. The steps below show what we do on a typical visit.

  1. Shut offWe turn off power or gas and close the water supply to the unit.
  2. ConnectHoses and a small pump attach to the isolation valves on the cold and hot sides.
  3. CirculateDescaling solution runs through the heat exchanger for the time the manufacturer lists.
  4. RinseWe flush clean water through, clean the inlet filter and check the water flow.
  5. Restart and testWe reopen the valves, restart the unit and check temperature and codes.

How often depends on your water and your model, so follow the manufacturer's schedule. Units without isolation valves can still be flushed, but valves make the job easier. We can add them during a visit.

Ask your utility about hardness and test your tap

The City of Saratoga Springs 2024 drinking water report lists total dissolved solids from 144 to 672 ppm. That range is wide, and it is not the same as hardness. The report lists no hardness value. Scale depends on hardness, so total dissolved solids alone do not tell you how fast it builds.

Ask your water utility for its annual water quality report, and test your tap with a hardness kit. USGS classes run from soft up to very hard above 180 mg/L as calcium carbonate. A harder tap means more frequent flushing, and a conditioner or softener may be worth discussing. See our water softener and filtration page.

Installing a tankless water heater

A tankless install is bigger than a tank swap. The unit needs the right gas line size or electrical capacity, a vent that can carry exhaust outside, a condensate drain for high-efficiency models and room on the wall. Isolation valves go on the water connections so the unit can be flushed later.

We check these before quoting, and permit rules vary by job, so ask the Saratoga Springs building department before work starts. If your tank is leaking right now, water heater replacement covers the faster options.

Repairing a tankless unit

Common repairs include cleaning the inlet filter, replacing a flame sensor or igniter on gas models, replacing a flow or temperature sensor, clearing a blocked condensate line and replacing a control board. We read the code, test the part and confirm the repair with a heat test.

If the heat exchanger is cracked or badly scaled, a repair may cost close to a new unit. We tell you early, before the work grows.

When tankless is not the answer

A tankless heater will not fix weak water pressure, and it will not give unlimited hot water if three fixtures run at once on a small unit. In very hard water without a plan for flushing, it can fail early. A tank or a hybrid unit may suit some homes better. See water heater installation for the full set of options.
