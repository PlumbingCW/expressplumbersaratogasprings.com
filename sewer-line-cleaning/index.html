Emergency Sewer Line Cleaning in Saratoga Springs, UT

When every drain in the house is slow, the main line is the suspect. Our plumbers clear sewer lines in Saratoga Springs from the cleanout out to the street.

Updated 2026-10-15 · Written by the Saratoga Springs Express Plumbing team

Sewer line cleaning in Saratoga Springs, UT is the clearing of the main drain that carries all of a home's wastewater out to the city sewer, usually through a cleanout and a cable machine or a hydro-jet. When this line clogs, tubs gurgle, toilets back up and floor drains overflow. The 24/7 emergency line is open if that is happening in your house tonight, and a plumber can be sent out with a main line machine.

While you wait, stop running water, keep the washer and dishwasher off, and do not flush. Keep people and pets out of any standing wastewater. After-hours and weekend calls are dispatched the same way as weekday ones, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

Is it the main line? A quick way to tell

The city's sewer page says backups inside a building are generally caused by blockages in the building's own piping. Saratoga Springs Public Works will inspect the sewer in the street to verify. That gives you a good order of operations.

When a main line backup is an emergency

Water rising in a tub or a floor drain means the main line is blocked. The wastewater will keep rising with every flush or shower. In a lower-level bathroom or basement the water can reach the floor in minutes.

Call the 24/7 line before you try a plunger on a full tub. Pour no chemical opener into a line that is full and move rugs and boxes off the floor. Late-night calls are routine for this work, and we bring a main line machine to the first visit.

Finding your cleanout

A cleanout is a capped pipe, usually white or black plastic, that gives a cable direct access to the main line. It is most often outside near the foundation, in a garage, in a basement or in a crawlspace near where the main drain leaves the house.

If you cannot find one, tell us. We may have to pull a toilet or work from a roof vent, which takes longer. A cleanout makes the job simpler and is also where a camera goes in, so knowing where yours is helps whoever works on it next.

Cables, cutters and jets for a main line

A sink or tub line uses a small cable. A main line carries more waste through a larger pipe, so we use a heavier machine with a longer cable and a cutter head sized to the pipe. A four-inch main gets a different head than a small branch.

Cutters break up paper and grease and chop roots. If the camera or the cable shows heavy root growth or grease along the whole pipe wall, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall rather than just opening a channel through it.

How a main line cleaning visit goes

  1. Find the cleanoutWe pull the cap carefully. If the line is full, the water level tells us the blockage is downstream.
  2. Cable the blockageThe cable machine feeds in until we reach the clog, then cuts through it and clears debris.
  3. Look with the cameraA camera pass shows whether the cable left grease, roots, a belly or a crack behind.
  4. Run water to testWe run water and flush fixtures to confirm the flow before we leave.

Storm drain and yard drain cleaning

A storm drain or a yard drain, such as a catch basin or a downspout line, collects rainwater and moves it away from the house. Leaves, soil and roots clog them, and a clog sends water against the foundation or across a patio.

We cable or jet these lines in the same way. They are separate from the sanitary sewer, so check which line a drain connects to before you pour anything down it. If a yard drain keeps backing up, our basement waterproofing page explains how drainage connects to plumbing.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

A short list for the waiting time

  • Stop all water use, including flushing, the shower and the washer
  • Locate the cleanout so the plumber does not have to search
  • Keep children and pets away from standing wastewater
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics off the wet area
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup for your insurer
