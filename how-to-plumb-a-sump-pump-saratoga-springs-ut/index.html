How to Plumb a Sump Pump: Saratoga Springs, UT Guide

A sump pump system has four parts: a basin, a pump, a check valve and a discharge line. This guide explains how they connect and which parts to leave to a plumber.

Water control · 7 min read · Updated 2026-10-15 · Written by the Saratoga Springs Express Plumbing team

Call (385) 488-1892

To plumb a sump pump in Saratoga Springs, UT, set a basin below the floor where water collects and place the pump in it. Run a discharge pipe up and out of the house, and add a check valve so water cannot flow back. The details that cause trouble are where the discharge ends, how the pipe is supported and whether the pump has power in a storm.

Below, each part is explained in the order water moves through it, followed by a table of what homeowners can do and what a plumber should handle.

Follow the water: basin, pump, check valve, discharge

1. The basin

A pit set into the floor, lined so it holds water without silting up. Water drains or seeps into it, and the pump sits at the bottom.

2. The pump

A submersible pump switches on when a float rises, and pushes water up through the discharge pipe. A pedestal pump sits above the pit on a motor stem.

3. The check valve

A one-way valve in the discharge pipe just above the pump. It stops the water in the pipe from running back into the basin when the pump shuts off.

4. The discharge

A pipe that carries water out through the wall and away from the foundation. The outlet end should deliver water where it will not flow back toward the house.

A small hole drilled in the discharge pipe below the check valve lets trapped air escape so the pump does not lose its prime. Many pump manuals call for it, so follow your manual.

The discharge line is where most mistakes happen

The discharge pipe moves water away from the house. If it ends too close to the foundation, the water you pumped out can soak back into the soil beside the wall and return to the basin. A longer run with a gentle downward slope helps the water drain away.

Do not tie the discharge to a drinking water line or to the irrigation system. The city says cross-connections between culinary and irrigation lines are illegal. For storm and discharge questions, ask Saratoga Springs Public Works where discharge may go, because we have not verified a city rule on that.

Power: the weak point of any pump

A sump pump needs electricity, and storms cause outages. A battery backup or a water-powered backup keeps the pump working when the power fails. Test any backup the way the manual says.

Plug the primary pump into its own outlet, and avoid sharing the circuit with a freezer or other heavy loads. If the breaker trips, the pump stops without warning.

Where sump pumps usually fail

Check these when the pump will not keep up

  • A float that sticks against the basin wall, so the pump never starts
  • A check valve installed backward or stuck shut
  • A basin full of silt or gravel that clogs the intake
  • A discharge pipe that freezes or kinks, or ends against the foundation
  • A tripped breaker or a dead battery in the backup

Sizing and the float switch

Pump size is about how much water must move and how high it must lift to reach the outlet, and the maker's performance chart shows both. A pump that is too small runs without catching up, and one that is oversized may short-cycle in a small basin.

The float switch sets when the pump turns on and off. It needs free room to rise and fall, so a basin that is too narrow can jam it. A plumber checks float travel when installing or replacing a pump.

Supports, unions and noise

A plastic discharge pipe is heavy when full of water and should be supported along the wall, not left hanging from the pump. A union fitting above the check valve lets you remove the pump later without cutting pipe.

Pumps hum and rattle if the pipe touches a joist or the basin. A bit of space and a rubber isolator quiet the system and prevent a slow leak at a joint.

Pump, waterproofing or both

A sump pump moves water out. It does not stop water from entering. If your basement keeps taking on water, the pump is the last line of defense and the cause may be drainage, grading or cracks.

Saratoga Springs: irrigation season and your discharge

The City of Saratoga Springs begins charging the pressurized irrigation system the week of April 15, and it is drained the week of October 15. Spring is when more water is applied around homes, so test your pump before the system is charged and note the dates when your basin runs. A pump that cycles right after sprinklers start can point to irrigation as the water source.

The irrigation connection is a green box marked irrigation near the property line, usually with a 1-inch ball valve. Keep your sump discharge away from that box and from the foundation, and ask Saratoga Springs Public Works about where storm and sump discharge may go before you route a pipe.
