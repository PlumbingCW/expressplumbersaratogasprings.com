Emergency Commercial Plumbing in Saratoga Springs, UT

When a drain backs up or a pipe leaks after closing, a business cannot wait for Monday. Our plumbers handle after-hours commercial calls in Saratoga Springs.

Updated 2026-10-15 · Written by the Saratoga Springs Express Plumbing team

Call (385) 488-1892

Commercial plumbing in Saratoga Springs, UT is service work for shops, offices, restaurants, rentals and multi-unit buildings, including drain clearing, leak repair, floor drain overflow and lift pump problems. Our licensed plumbers dispatch 24/7, so a call after closing time gets the same answer as one during business hours. We are a repair and service team, not a build-out contractor, and this page lists what we do and what we hand off.

Until a plumber arrives, close the nearest shutoff or the building's main valve, stop using drains and keep staff and customers away from wet floors and electrical equipment. Move stock and records out of the water path. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and nights and weekends are answered.

What we do for commercial properties, and what we do not

Our commercial work is repair and service. We clear main lines and branch drains, repair leaking pipes and fixtures, fix floor drain overflow and sump or lift pump trouble, replace water heaters and connect fixtures. We use cable machines, drain cameras and, when needed, hydro-jetting for stubborn lines.

There are clear limits. We do not pump or haul grease trap waste, install or service fire sprinkler systems, do medical gas piping, take on large new-construction build-outs, or do electrical and structural work. When your problem falls outside plumbing repair, we tell you on the first call and point you to the right trade.

Typical calls we take

  • A restaurant, shop or office with a main line that backs up
  • A rental or multi-unit building with a leak or a burst pipe
  • A floor drain that overflows or smells of sewage
  • A lift pump or sump that has stopped or alarmed
  • A broken fixture, valve or water heater in a business

Drains, grates and floor drains

Commercial drains see more load than home drains. A kitchen, restroom or mop sink may carry grease, debris, paper towels or wipes into the same line. When a floor drain is overflowing, the cause is usually in the branch or main, not the grate.

We clear the line with a cable or jet, remove stuck or locked grates and check the flow with a camera when the problem repeats. If the line backs up into the dining room, treat it as urgent and call the same night.

When a business plumbing failure is an emergency

Some failures cannot wait for morning. Water in a store, a restroom that cannot be used or a drain that backs up into a kitchen can close a business and damage inventory, flooring and ceilings.

Call the 24/7 line immediately for

  • Water spraying or pouring from a pipe or fixture
  • Sewage or gray water rising in a floor drain, restroom or kitchen
  • A water heater leaking onto the floor
  • A lift pump alarm or an overflowing sump pit
  • No water at all in a restaurant or building that is open to the public

Close the nearest valve or the main, stop using the affected drains and turn off power to wet equipment if you can reach the breaker without standing in water. Photograph the damage for the insurance claim. The dispatcher asks what is affected and arranges the earliest visit the schedule allows.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Close the nearest valve or the main water shutoff
  • Stop using the affected drains and restrooms
  • Turn off power to wet equipment only if you can reach the breaker dry
  • Photograph the damage before anything is moved

Grease trap cleaning: what we do and what we refer

Callers often ask whether we pump grease traps. We do not pump or haul trap waste, because that is usually a separate trade with its own trucks and disposal requirements. Ask a licensed hauler for pumping and the required waste handling.

What we do is the plumbing around it. We clear the lines running to and from the trap, find out why it backs up, and repair or replace damaged fittings and cleanouts. A trap that overflows soon after pumping can also have a blockage past it, and a camera tells you whether the line needs work.

Commercial leak detection

When a commercial water bill rises with no visible leak, the problem may be a hidden pipe, a running fixture or a failing valve. We use meter checks, pressure tests and acoustic listening. Our leak detection page explains the methods. If the leak turns out to be in a pipe the building does not own, we say so.

Property managers, landlords and tenants

Property managers can call the same 24/7 line for a rental unit, a multi-unit building or a commercial space. Tell us who authorizes the work and who pays, since lease terms differ. We give a clear price before work starts and a plain description of what was found.

We do not decide who is responsible under a lease. We describe the cause, such as grease, roots, wipes or a failed valve, so you and the tenant can sort that out. For a single bad fixture, a fixture repair call is often enough.

What affects the price of commercial plumbing

Scope and size

A single drain is a small job. A main line or multiple floor drains takes more time and equipment.

Access

Locked grates, ceiling access and tight utility rooms add time.

Cause and condition

Grease, debris and roots call for jetting or repair beyond a cable.

Parts and equipment

Commercial valves, pumps and water heaters differ in cost from household parts.

Time of day

After-hours and weekend calls are answered at any hour. Ask for the clear price before work starts.

A note on insurance

A commercial property policy may cover sudden water damage but often not the plumbing repair or gradual leaks. Check with your insurer or agent, and keep photos, the plumber's notes and receipts.
