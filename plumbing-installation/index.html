Plumbing Installation in Saratoga Springs, UT

New fixtures, lines and gas piping are planned jobs, and they go better with a clear scope. Our licensed plumbers handle installations across Saratoga Springs.

Updated 2026-10-15 · Written by the Saratoga Springs Express Plumbing team

Call (385) 488-1892

Plumbing installation in Saratoga Springs, UT is the planned work of adding or replacing fixtures, water lines, drain lines, gas piping and valves so they work and pass the checks that apply to your job. Our licensed and insured plumbers do this by appointment, and our 24/7 dispatch line is also open if a plumbing problem turns urgent.

Most installs begin with a walk-through. We look at the existing pipes, ask what you want to change and give a clear price before work starts. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

Installing fixtures and supply lines

A new faucet, toilet, sink, shower valve or dishwasher connection looks simple, but the details decide whether it leaks. We check the shutoff valves, replace worn supply lines and match the fitting to the pipe. Old valves that no longer close fully get replaced while the water is off.

For a swap, we remove the old fixture, clean the connection and test for leaks before we leave. For a new location, we run supply and drain lines and set the slope and venting. If the fixture itself is failing, faucet and sink repair and toilet repair explain when repair is enough.

Remodel rough-in: plumbing before the walls close

Rough-in is the pipework that goes in before drywall: supply lines, drains, vents and stub-outs for fixtures. It sets where every sink, shower and toilet can go, so it is worth getting right on paper first.

We work from your layout and check how the new lines connect to the existing ones. Bring plans, fixture models and any tile or cabinet details. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Saratoga Springs building department before work starts.

What to bring to a rough-in planning visit

  • Fixture list and model numbers
  • Floor plan showing where each fixture sits
  • Whether walls or floors will be open and for how long
  • Where the main shutoff and cleanouts are
  • Whether you plan to add a gas line

Gas lines: safety first, then the work

If you smell gas

Leave the building first. Do not flip switches, use a phone or light anything inside. Once outside, call the gas utility. In Utah, Enbridge Gas lists 1-800-767-1689 for gas emergencies. You can also call 911.

This page covers gas line piping work, appliance connections and inspections. It is not an emergency gas repair promise, and we do not make any promise about how fast a gas response will arrive. For a smell of gas, the utility and 911 come first.

For planned work, we install and connect gas piping for ranges, dryers, water heaters, fireplaces and outdoor grills. We also inspect existing lines when you buy a home or add an appliance. A gas line inspection checks the visible piping, connections and shutoffs. Ask what the inspection covers and what it does not, and ask for a written note if you need one.

If we find a suspected leak during a visit, we stop, shut off the gas and tell you. Gas appliance questions also come up on water heater installation, where the gas connection is part of the job.

Backflow preventers and what testing involves

A backflow preventer is a device that stops used or contaminated water from flowing backward into the drinking water supply. You often find one on an irrigation system, a fire line or a commercial service. Because irrigation water in Saratoga Springs is non-potable and culinary and irrigation lines must never connect, backflow protection matters.

Who must test a device, how often and who may certify it vary. Ask the city or your water supplier what applies to your property. We do not state testing rules from memory.

If you have received a notice that a valve was locked or a test is due, call the water supplier first to learn the requirement. We can help with the plumbing side: installing, repairing or replacing a preventer and arranging the test with a qualified tester. For businesses, see commercial plumbing.

Outdoor spigots and hose bibs

A hose bib is the outdoor faucet. It can leak at the handle, drip inside the wall or lose its anti-siphon feature. Winter is the time to disconnect hoses and drain the line, since water left in an exposed spigot can freeze.

We replace worn bibs, add frost-resistant models where they suit, and install new ones for gardens, wash areas or outdoor kitchens. Remember that irrigation connections are separate from the house hose bib and must never be tied to the culinary line.

Pressure reducing valves described plainly

A pressure reducing valve sits where the main line enters the home and lowers incoming pressure to a level the fixtures can handle. When it wears out, you may see banging pipes, leaking fixtures or weak flow. We test it, adjust it if it can be adjusted and replace it when it cannot. We do not quote pressure numbers here since the right setting depends on your home and your supply.

New construction plumbing

For new homes and additions, plumbing comes in stages: underground drains, rough-in, then trim when fixtures go on. Coordination with the builder and inspections matters at each stage. Ask the Saratoga Springs building department what inspections apply. Our water line replacement work covers the service line side when it needs upgrading.
