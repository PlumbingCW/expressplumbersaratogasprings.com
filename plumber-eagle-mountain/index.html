What a city built in two decades does to its plumbing

A house built in the last twenty years usually has a clean plumbing layout and no history of patched pipe. It also has parts that were installed once and never touched again, such as the water heater, the shutoff valves under every sink and the supply connections behind the toilets.

Cities this young also spread out fast, with long runs of pipe between the meter and a house set back on a large lot. A small leak on a long water line can run for weeks before a wet patch shows, so we start with a pressure check and a meter test.

The city counted 21,415 residents in 2010 on the way to 43,623 in 2020. For you, that means a plumber should ask how old the water heater is and where the shutoff sits, instead of guessing at the age of the pipe.

Water from five wells and a surface supply: what that means at your tap

The city runs its own water department and its 2025 water quality report lists five groundwater wells plus surface water from the Central Utah Water Conservancy District. Total dissolved solids ran from 148 to 1,320 ppm across that supply. The report lists no hardness value.

TDS counts every dissolved mineral and salt, so a high reading is a reason to test, not proof of hard water. If you see white crust on faucets or a tank that rumbles when it heats, test your tap for hardness and ask the city water department for its annual report. A water heater that scales early is a common reason for a replacement call.

Leaks and water line trouble in houses under twenty-five years old

Newer homes leak in predictable places. A supply connection behind a toilet weeps, a fitting at the water heater loosens after repeated heating cycles, or the line from the meter to the house develops a pinhole where it was bedded. A running meter with every fixture off is the clearest sign.

We start with a pressure test, then use acoustic listening gear and a pipe locator to place the leak before any digging. Our leak detection work is built for exactly this kind of hidden loss. If the leak turns out to be on the buried line, water line repair follows, and we tell you the options before we start.

Call 811 at least three business days before any digging. Eagle Mountain's own page lists a frost depth of 30 inches, so ask the city how that applies to your job before a trench is opened.

Water heaters in Cedar Valley houses

A tank that leaks from the bottom cannot be repaired, because the steel liner has failed. Our water heater replacement team can usually plan same-day work when the schedule allows, and after-hours calls are taken at any time.

Utah emergency guidance recommends securing your water heater to the wall, and Eagle Mountain lists residential seismic design category D2 on its own design-criteria page. Check that your tank has strapping and know where the gas or power shutoff is. If you are replacing the tank anyway, ask for the strapping to be included in the work.

Drains in newer houses are cleaner but not clog-proof

A modern drain line is smooth plastic with few bends, so clogs tend to be recent and soft: kitchen grease, bath hair and products labeled flushable that never break down. A cable machine clears most of them in one visit. When the lowest drain gurgles first, the blockage is in the main house drain, and we run a camera to confirm the pipe is intact.

Our drain cleaning page covers the tools and how we prove the line is flowing before we leave.

Before we arrive: a shutoff plan for a newer Eagle Mountain house

Before we arrive

Find the main valve now

In many newer houses the main shutoff sits in a garage wall, a utility closet or near the water heater. Turn it clockwise until it stops, then open a low faucet to drain the lines.

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near an outlet, a garage freezer or the water heater's electrical supply, turn off that breaker at the panel. Do not step in standing water to reach it.

Clear the area

Lift rugs, boxes and electronics off wet floors and move kids and pets away. Photograph the damage for your insurer before you move large items.
