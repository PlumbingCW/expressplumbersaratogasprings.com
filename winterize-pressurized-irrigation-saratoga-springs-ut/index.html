Pressurized Irrigation Winterization in Saratoga Springs, UT

The city drains the pressurized irrigation system the week of October 15. Leave your valves open then, close the ball valve after November 1, and keep it separate from your drinking water plumbing.

Seasonal plumbing · 7 min read · Updated 2026-10-15 · Written by the Saratoga Springs Express Plumbing team

Call (385) 488-1892

To winterize pressurized irrigation in Saratoga Springs, leave your individual shut-off valves open while the city drains the system the week of October 15, then close your individual ball valve after November 1. The city charges the system the week of April 15 and drains it the week of October 15, and in between it is inactive. This page covers the plumbing side: hose bibs, backflow, the separation of irrigation and drinking water lines, and finding leaks in spring.

Pressurized irrigation is non-potable water used only outdoors, and it runs in its own system. Your drinking water plumbing is a different system with different freeze risks, and the two should never be connected.

Why you leave the valves open in fall

An open valve lets water drain out of the lines as the city drains the system. A closed valve can trap water in your piping, and water that freezes expands and can crack fittings and valve bodies.

The ball valve is the final step, and the city says to close it after November 1. Closing it earlier risks trapping water before the drain is finished.

Hose bibs and other drinking water lines outside

Your outdoor hose bibs are on the drinking water system, not the irrigation system, and they need their own winter steps. Disconnect hoses and splitters before cold weather, since a hose left on holds water against the faucet.

If your bib has an interior shut-off, close it and open the bib outside to drain the short run. A bib that drips all winter, or one that sprays inside the wall once it thaws, is a burst pipe waiting to happen. Call us right away if you find water where it should not be.

Backflow and keeping the two systems separate

A backflow device stops water from running backward from a sprinkler line into a drinking water line. It is a safety piece. If you have one, protect it from freezing the same way you would any exposed fitting, and ask the city or a licensed plumber about testing.

Never connect irrigation piping to your drinking water plumbing. The city says cross connections between the culinary and irrigation lines are illegal. Common mistakes are a hose bib tied to a sprinkler manifold or a quick connector linking the two.

Finding irrigation leaks in spring

After the system is charged, walk each zone. A soggy patch on a dry day, a head that geysers, or a hiss at the valve box usually points to a break. Heads and fittings that cracked over winter are common culprits.

  1. Turn off every zoneClose the zone valves and watch the lawn. Wet spots that persist point to a leak in a main line.
  2. Check the valve boxOpen the green box near the property line and listen for running water with the ball valve open and all zones off.
  3. Test one zone at a timeRun each zone and look for broken heads, low spots and misted pipe joints.
  4. Mark and compareNote where water surfaces. A string of wet spots along a line suggests a pipe break between them.

Mistakes that cost homeowners a repair

Closing the valve before the drain week

The city asks you to leave individual shut-off valves open so the system drains.

Leaving hoses on hose bibs

A hose holds water against the faucet, and that water can freeze and split the bib.

Linking irrigation to house plumbing

Cross connections are illegal and can put non-potable water in your drinking supply.

Forgetting where the green box is

You need the location to shut the ball valve in November and check it in spring.

Skipping the spring leak walk

A broken line waters one spot for weeks before the lawn shows it.

Signs a freeze damaged something

Check after the first hard freeze

  • A hose bib that drips or sprays inside the wall when water is turned on
  • A wet spot in a garage or crawl space on a cold morning
  • Reduced flow at one fixture after a hard freeze
  • Water hammer, a bang when a valve closes, in lines that run outside
  • An unexplained jump on the water meter with every fixture off

  • Disconnect hoses and close the interior shut-off to outdoor bibs
  • Do not connect irrigation and drinking water lines

Saratoga Springs: the city's rules for pressurized irrigation

The City's Pressurized Irrigation page says this is non-potable water that should never be used for drinking, cooking or playing in. Keep kids and pets away from it, and do not fill pools or drinking bowls from it.

The page says irrigation systems should not be turned on between 10 AM and 6 PM. Set your timers outside that window so you are following the city and not running sprinklers through the hottest part of the day.

Homeowner connections are in a green box marked irrigation near the property line, usually a 1-inch ball valve. Learn where it is now, because that is your fall and spring control point.
